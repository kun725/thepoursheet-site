FAIR QUESTIONS
Why wouldn't I just pull these filings myself?
You could. They're public, spread across four agencies that don't reference each other, and they change daily. The sheet is those four reads done weekly and keyed by address, so a liquor application, a buildout permit, and a health inspection at the same storefront show up as one venue instead of three disconnected rows. The real question is whether your week has room for reading dockets.
What's in the full edition that the sample doesn't show?
The sample is six worked-up venues. The full edition adds the complete list for your borough as spreadsheet rows: every new on-premises filing, recently 50 to 100 a month across Manhattan, Brooklyn and Queens, each with its dates, stage, and buildout dollars where filed.
Will my competitors read the same sheet?
Not if you take the exclusive seat. One per category per borough: one POS dealer, one distributor, one broker. First come.
How do I know the stage reads hold up?
You grade them. The trial month logs every read for your borough, and at the end you compare the sheet's calls against what actually happened. If it was wrong, you found out for free.
